The direct answer

"Which AI search can read my website?" is really three questions stacked together: what index does the engine use, how fast does it pick up a new page, and does it need you to rank first? Here is the honest version for each of the four engines in 2026.

  • ChatGPT Search: let its search crawler reach your public pages, return the useful content in HTML and give each page a clear title. Check your crawler settings with our free crawler checker.
  • Perplexity runs its own crawler and index and tends to favor fresher pages. Check the current result for your page after publishing a change.
  • Keep your pages discoverable. Publish your page URLs in a sitemap, check crawler access and review indexing in Search Console.
  • Claude answers from live retrieval (RAG), which is independent of training data. A freshly indexed page can be cited without ever appearing in any model's training set.

Make the page useful on the first read. Put the answer near the top, make it available in the initial HTML, and use clear headings and structured data so a reader or retrieval tool can extract it.

Check page access and index state

Being crawlable is necessary but not sufficient. A page can allow every AI crawler and still get ignored because nothing on it is easy to extract. When we scored 189 well-known companies across 10 industries (0 to 100) on six fundamentals (AI-crawler access, JSON-LD structured data, title/meta, Open Graph, XML sitemap, and llms.txt), the gaps were surprising.

OpenAI and GitHub both scored a D on their own homepages: JS-heavy, thin server-rendered content. Perplexity (an AI search engine) scored a C. LlamaIndex, whose entire product is making data readable by LLMs, scored a D. And Klarna scored an F (38, the lowest). Ars Technica also scored an F: it allows crawlers but ships no structured data, so there is nothing clean for an engine to lift.

Prepare pages a reader can use

The peer-reviewed GEO work out of Princeton and Georgia Tech (KDD 2024) is blunt about what raises your odds of being cited by an LLM:

  • Adding statistics: about +32% visibility.
  • Direct quotations: about +41%.
  • Citing named sources: about +30%.
  • Answer-first wins: about 44% of LLM citations come from the first 30% of a page. Put the answer up top, like we did.

What to do this week

  1. Keep your pages discoverable. Publish your page URLs in a sitemap, check crawler access and review indexing in Search Console.
  2. Open the gates for AI crawlers. Confirm GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and Google-Extended are allowed in robots.txt. Blocking them means zero citations, full stop.
  3. Ship server-rendered, extractable content. If a JS-heavy homepage earned OpenAI and GitHub a D, it can do the same to you. Make sure your core copy renders without JavaScript.
  4. Add the schema that counts. Organization + Article on content pages, SoftwareApplication for tools, Service + Offer for paid items.
  5. Rewrite answer-first. Lead with the direct answer, add a real statistic, a direct quote, and a named source in the first 30%.
