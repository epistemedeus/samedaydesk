What actually goes wrong

These are the defects owners bring us, in the words they use. Every one of them has a source page or a listing behind it.

  • A stale price. The number an engine quotes came off a page you changed, a directory you forgot, or a reseller who never updated.
  • A service you retired. The answer still sells it, so the call you take is for work you do not do.
  • Closed when you are open. Hours or a closure that survived on one listing and got repeated everywhere.
  • Someone else's reviews. The answer summarises complaints that belong to a business with a similar name.
  • A phone number that is not yours. Sometimes a rival's, sometimes invented.
  • Mixed up with a similar name. Two businesses collapse into one entity, and the wrong one gets the credit.
  • Sending buyers to a rival. You are absent from the answer that decides the shortlist.

What we do about it

  1. Check. The same frozen list of buyer questions, asked of named engines, more than once, logged out. What came back is quoted verbatim with a timestamp.
  2. Find the source. For each wrong answer, the page or listing that still says it. When the link is inferred rather than cited, the report says suspected source and shows why.
  3. Correct what you own. A canonical fact page, valid structured data, crawler access, and the specific first-party pages that carry the wrong fact.
  4. Submit what you do not own. Directory and profile mismatches get filed, with a screenshot of each ticket. Filed is not the same as accepted, and we never report it as accepted.
  5. Measure again. The same questions, the same way, at day 10 and again at day 30 and day 90, with the before and after side by side.

We do not control what a model outputs. We control what your pages say, what the directories say, and what a crawler is allowed to read.

Questions owners ask

Why does Google say my business is closed when we are open?

Almost always because one record says so and the rest of the graph copied it. A single stale listing, a temporary closure that was never lifted, a duplicate profile, or a data aggregator that still carries the old status. An AI overview summarises whatever the strongest records agree on, so one loud wrong record can outvote your own site. The fix is to find every record that carries the status, correct the ones you control, and file corrections on the ones you do not.

How do I fix wrong information about my company in ChatGPT?

You cannot edit the model, and there is no console where you type the correction. What you can change is the material it reads: your own pages, your structured data, your listings, and whether its crawlers are allowed in at all. Then you wait for a refetch. That is the whole mechanism, and anyone who describes a faster route is describing something they cannot do.

What do I do when the AI overview uses another company's reviews?

That is usually an entity problem rather than a review problem: two businesses with similar names, or a shared address history, have merged into one entity in the graph. Correcting reviews will not fix it. Making your entity unambiguous will: consistent legal name and trading name, one canonical page that states who you are, structured data that matches it, and disambiguation filed with the places that merged you.

How do I check what ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google actually say about my business?

Ask them the questions your buyers ask, logged out, more than once, and write down what came back with the date. Answers vary between runs, so a single screenshot is not evidence of anything. The free report on this page does the short version for you, and the methods page publishes the full protocol so you can run it yourself.

Why is ChatGPT sending customers to my competitors?

Two different causes need two different answers. Either the engine cannot read you well enough to have an opinion, which is a crawler, structured data, and canonical-page problem, or it can read you and still prefers someone else, which is a question about reviews, third-party coverage, and whether your pages answer the question at all. We will tell you which one you have. We will not promise to reverse the second one.

I already updated my Google listing. Why is the AI overview still wrong?

Because the listing is one input among many, and the answer you are looking at may predate your edit. Caches, index refresh cycles, aggregator copies, and third-party pages that repeat the old fact all keep it alive. This is why we correct every source we can find rather than one, and why the re-measurement happens at day 30 and day 90 instead of the day after the edit.

Can I tell Google or OpenAI the answer is wrong?

There are feedback controls and removal forms, and they are worth using, but there is no support queue where a business owner gets a wrong answer corrected on request. Owners who have tried report the same thing: the form is submitted and the text stays live. The reliable lever is the material behind the answer, not an appeal.

How long until the wrong answer actually changes?

It depends on the surface, and we refuse to publish one blended number. A page an engine fetches at answer time can reflect an edit within days. An index-backed answer waits for a recrawl. Something the model absorbed during training may keep repeating regardless of what your site says today. We measure at day 30 and day 90 and show you which surfaces moved.

What if AI mixes my business up with a company that has a similar name?

That is an entity collision, and it is the hardest class on this list. The work is to make your identity unambiguous everywhere you control and file disambiguation where you do not. It is also on the printed list of things the sprint acceptance rule never fires for, because the other business exists and we cannot delete it.

Do I need a special "AI" service, or is this just keeping my information consistent?

Mostly it is keeping your information consistent, done thoroughly and proved with dated evidence. The part that is genuinely new is the measurement: knowing which questions to ask, which surfaces to ask them on, and how to trace a wrong sentence back to the page it came from. If a vendor tells you it is a new discipline requiring a monthly subscription, be careful.
