Terms

These are the rules that decide when a clock starts, when money comes back, and what counts as done. They are on this page because a rule you find out about afterwards is not a rule, it is a surprise.

Last updated 2026-08-19. Neomorphic LLC, trading as SameDayDesk, Sheridan, Wyoming, United States.

1. Business days

Every clock on this site counts business days: Monday to Friday in America/Los_Angeles, excluding US federal holidays. Weekends and federal holidays are not counted. We publish this because the delivery time is part of what you are buying.

2. When the audit clock starts

The AI Answer Audit is delivered within 5 business days, counted from the next business morning after your intake is complete. Complete intake means: the website address, the brand name buyers say, the delivery email, your primary city or market, the category word buyers type, one flagship service, three competitors, five to ten buyer questions, and at least one canonical fact.

The clock starts when that form is complete, not when Stripe succeeds.

3. When the sprint clock starts

The Answer Correction Sprint is delivered within 15 business days, and Correction Sprint Plus within 20, counted from the next business morning after all three of these are true: the intake is complete, the access checklist is green or you have ticked the public-only waiver, and you have approved the frozen change list.

Payment alone starts nothing. We would rather hold the clock than start one we cannot honour.

4. Pause

The delivery clock pauses while we are waiting on information, access, an approval, or an answer to a clarifying question. It resumes on the next business morning after the missing item arrives. We send one question rather than guessing, and we say in that email that the clock is paused.

5. Refund before work starts

Full refund, no questions, at any point before work begins. Work begins when the clock starts.

6. Instrument A: the audit refund

A cash refund of the $490, claimable in writing within 14 days of delivery. The report stays yours either way. It fires when any one of these is true:

  1. Files are missing or cannot be opened and parsed.
  2. The report contains no defect a specialist would recognise as one.
  3. The report missed a competitor you named that the engines recommend on the panel questions.
  4. A fact you gave us in the intake is contradicted in the answers and is not logged in the register.

It never fires because an engine did not cite you, did not rank you, or did not name you. Nobody controls that.

If a wrong address, a wrong entity, or a wrong phone number in your intake caused a miss, that miss is not class 4. We will show you the intake value we used.

7. Instrument B: the sprint credit

The $490 audit fee applies in full to a sprint started within 30 days of audit delivery. After 30 days the credit expires and the files stay yours. A full cash refund under instrument A extinguishes the credit; you cannot have both.

8. Instrument C: sprint acceptance

A sprint is accepted on artifacts, not on model output. The artifacts are: the canonical fact page live, structured data valid, crawler access corrected where it was blocking, the named first-party defects on the frozen list closed, third-party mismatches submitted with a ticket screenshot, and the day 10 measurement delivered.

At day 30 we re-measure on a de-personalized resample, three runs per question, logged out. The acceptance rule fires when the engine still quotes the old first-party string and cites a URL this sprint edited. That is the case where our work did not do what it was supposed to do, and we keep working at no charge until it does or until we tell you honestly that we cannot.

The rule never fires for any of these, and they are listed here rather than discovered later:

  • Entity collision with a similarly named business.
  • Best-of lists and roundups.
  • Reviews and review aggregators.
  • Answers that come from training memory with no source cited.
  • Aggregator knowledge graphs.
  • Franchise or directory name, address, and phone records the client does not own.
  • Answers produced with search turned off.

The day 30 and day 90 warranty rechecks carry a two hour cap each. Work beyond that cap becomes a separate ticket, quoted in writing before it starts.

9. No outcome guarantee

Nobody controls what an AI model outputs. We do not promise a ranking, a citation, a mention, or a position, and we will not accept a brief that depends on one. What we promise is the work listed on the card you bought, delivered on the clock printed there.

10. What you own

You own the report, the structured data we write, the robots and crawler changes, the fact-page copy, and the screenshots. Our methods, our internal notes, and unused drafts stay ours. On a refund you keep whatever was already delivered.

You may not resell the report as your own product. If you are an agency buying for a client, tell us at intake and we will address the deliverable to them.

11. Revisions

Audit: one written round of factual corrections, within 5 business days of delivery. Sprint: one consolidated written round. Silence for 5 business days after delivery means accepted. Further rounds are a new ticket with a written price.

12. Change orders

Anything not on the frozen change list gets a written price and a written number of days before any work happens. The frozen list is capped at eight items on a sprint so that cap means something.

13. If we are late

If we miss a published delivery clock for a reason that is not a pause under clause 4 and not force majeure under clause 16, you choose: we finish with a new dated commitment at no extra charge, or you take a full refund.

14. Silence

If you pay and the intake stays incomplete, we send one reminder after 24 hours. After 10 business days of silence we write to close or extend. After 20 business days we refund in full, because work has not started. We do not keep money for work we did not do.

15. If you cancel after the clock starts

We invoice the work actually done at the published hourly equivalent behind each price, and refund the rest. We do not keep the whole fee for a partly delivered project.

16. Third parties and force majeure

Directory tickets, profile corrections, and index refreshes are submitted, not guaranteed accepted, and their timing is not ours to set. Force majeure covers an engine-wide or payment-processor outage, a named public holiday, or documented operator illness. Being busy is not force majeure.

17. Chargebacks

Use clause 6 or clause 13 first. Both are faster than a chargeback and neither requires you to argue with a bank.

18. Case-study rights

Nothing about your engagement is published without your written approval of an actual draft. The optional documentation boxes on the sprint intake are separate from each other, none of them changes the price, and none of them is conditioned on you saying something positive. Operative language for that instrument is with counsel; until it is settled, those boxes record a preference and nothing more.

19. The free report

The free report is for the domain you own or work on. It is not a competitor research service, and the question panel is fixed rather than open. One live run per domain per day; repeat visits return the stored copy with its timestamp. It is not a client engagement, and it is not the audit.

20. Privacy in one paragraph

We keep what you send us to do the work you paid for: the intake, the delivery address, and the order record. The free report stores the domain and the result, not you. We do not sell anything to anyone. Ask us to delete your record and we will, once the work and the refund window are closed.
